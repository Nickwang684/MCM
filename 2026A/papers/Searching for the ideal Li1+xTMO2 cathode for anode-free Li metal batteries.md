# Searching for the ideal Li1+xTMO2 cathode for anode-free Li metal batteries

## Abstract  

Anode-free [lithium metal batteries](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/lithium-metal-battery "Learn more about lithium metal batteries from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") push the [energy density](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/energy-density "Learn more about energy density from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") higher and minimize battery production costs as low as possible. However, the fast capacity decay impedes their commercial viability, primarily due to the lack of excessive Li from the anode to compensate for the irreversible lithium loss. Thus, the Li-rich NCM cathode is a feasible way to solve the issue. In this work, to search for the ideal Li1+xTMO2 cathode for anode-free Li metal batteries, we selected the two types of commonly used layered [cathode materials](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/cathode-material "Learn more about cathode materials from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") (LiTMO2: NCM622 and NCM811) to enrich Li converting into Li2TMO2 by both chemical lithiation (C-Li) and electrochemical lithiation (E-Li) methods. Our findings show that the Li-rich NCM622 lithiated by the E-Li method is an ideal choice among our candidates, which has a high lithiation degree that almost covers the entire reversible transition range from Li1 to Li2 without additional by-products and a negative impact on kinetic performance. Based on the above results, we further demonstrated that the Li1.33NCM622|Cu pouch cell presents a longer cycle life of >200 times with a high capacity retention of 74 %.  

## Introduction  

The global shift towards mobility, characterized by the widespread use of portable electronic devices such as smartphones, laptops, and wearable technology, has created a massive demand for compact, high-energy-density batteries [[1], [2], [3]]. Hence, lithium metal batteries (LMBs) are making a comeback due to their superior energy density, utilizing lithium metal as the anode, which has the lowest reduction potential (−3.04 V vs. Standard Hydrogen Electrode) and the highest theoretical capacity (3860 mAh/g) [[4], [5], [6]]. The substantial excess lithium, often hundreds of microns thick, used in most LMBs would negate the advantage of energy density [[7], [8], [9], [10], [11]]. However, manufacturing lithium sheets thinner than 100 µm is challenging and costly [12].  

Anode-free lithium metal batteries (AF-LMBs) pair the cathode directly with the Cu current collector without the initial Li metal anode, maximizing energy density at the cell level and simultaneously decreasing costs and processing complexity [[13], [14], [15]]. Upon recharging, lithium ions are plated onto the Cu collector, reforming the lithium metal anode [16]. Then, lithium ions are stripped from the anode during discharging and transferred back to the cathode. Therefore, there is no difference between AF-LMBs and LMBs regarding working mechanisms. Both have the same issue: infinite volume change, ongoing side reactions, and dendrite growth during Li plating/stripping [9,[17], [18], [19], [20]]. However, unlike LMBs, AF-LMBs suffer from more severe cycle life fading due to the lack of excessive lithium compensation, leading to any irreversible loss of active lithium directly reflecting on low coulomb efficiency and declining capacity [21]. It undoubtedly makes it a significant challenge for AF-LMBs to achieve a satisfactory cycle life.  

There has been a lot of work [14,17,[22], [23], [24]] on improving Li utilization efficiency including collector modification and electrolyte engineering, which has made significant achievements, but the improvement in the cycling performance of AF-LMBs is still limited (Figure S1). Given that the loss of active lithium primarily determines the cycle life of AF-LMBs, pre-lithiation of the cathode plays an another important role on extending the cycle life by offering additional lithium sources to compensate for the irreversible active lithium loss during the cycle [[25], [26], [27], [28], [29]]. Many traditional sacrificial lithium salts have been introduced into LIBs to compensate for the lithium consumed by SEI formation [[30], [31], [32], [33], [34], [35], [36], [37], [38]], as shown in Table S1. There have been successful cases of extending the life of AF-LMBs [25,39,40], such as Li2O and LiNO3, listed in Table S2. But the introduction of additional inactive substances or gases is unfavorable for the energy density and safety. It's necessary to introduce reversible lithium-rich compounds. Ni-rich layered cathodes (LiNixCoyMn1−_x_−yO2, LiTMO2, _x_0.6) typically demonstrate high capacity and cycle stability within an operating range of 2.2 to 4.3 V, making it widely favored for cathode materials [[41], [42], [43], [44]]. Furthermore, during the discharge voltage range of 2.2–1.2 V (over-lithiation), LiTMO2 with space group R-3 m (Li1) could convert to Li2TMO2 with P-3m1 (Li2) and almost reversibly convert back to the original structure during subsequent charging [[45], [46], [47], [48]]. Once the Li2TMO2 cathode is introduced, it can release nearly twice the lithium resources during the first cycle without generating any other inactive materials, thereby maximizing the energy density and cycle life simultaneously [27]. In previous works, the identification and electrochemical characterization of Li2NCM811 have been verified, and the structural evolution during multiple over-lithiation processes has also been investigated [27,49].

As in the previous literature, the cycling stability of AF-LMBs is mainly determined by the reversibility of the Li metal anode. In such a case, we can assume that the intrinsic capacity loss of Li1+xTMO2 is ignored, and the discharge capacity decay entirely depends on active lithium consumption. Assuming the structure stability of the cathode remains stable, a 30 % pre-lithiation (Li1.3NCM) could nearly double the number of cycles (151 cycles vs. 80 cycles) with 80 % capacity retention. In reality, as the degree of pre-lithiation increases, side effects such as lattice expansion and non-ideal phase transitions, tend to deteriorate the capacity and stability of the cathode [49]. Therefore, it is imperative to search an ideal lithium-rich cathode without degradation capacity exhibition or reduced cycling performance.  

Considering the superior crystallinity and structural integrity of single crystals [50], they may effectively prevent structural instability and severe fragmentation among secondary particles [51] caused by lattice mismatch and expansion during the introducing Li2 phase. Thus, in this work, we prefer single crystal cathodes with varied Ni contents, including Li1Ni0.6Co0.2Mn0.2O2 (NCM622) and Li1Ni0.8Co0.1Mn0.1O2 (NCM811) cathodes, and explore a series of lithiation degrees both with electrochemical (E-Li) and chemical lithiation (C-Li) methods. Among them, NCM622 shows outstanding lithiation ability, and Li1+xNCM622 obtained through the E-Li method exhibits stable electrochemical performance within a broader lithiation range. Additionally, we confirmed the effectiveness and superiority of pre-lithiation for the cathode in practical anode-free pouch cells, with an improved capacity retention of 80 % after 173 cycles.
