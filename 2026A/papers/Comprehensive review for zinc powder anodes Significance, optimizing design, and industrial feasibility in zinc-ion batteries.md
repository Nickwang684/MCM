# Comprehensive review for zinc powder anodes: Significance, optimizing design, and industrial feasibility in zinc-ion batteries

## Abstract  

Rechargeable aqueous zinc-ion batteries (AZIBs) have emerged as promising candidates for sustainable [energy storage systems](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/energy-storage-system "Learn more about energy storage systems from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages"), due to their low cost, enhanced safety, and high-power density. Nevertheless, practical applications are still hampered by inherent challenges related to the zinc (Zn) foil anode, including dendrite formation and interfacial parasitic reactions. As an alternative, Zn powder (Zn-p) presents significant potential, owing to its cost-efficiency, large-scale [processability](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/processability "Learn more about processability from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages"), versatility, and industrial applicability. Notably, the amount of Zn-p can be accurately regulated, enhancing [Zn anodes](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/zinc-anode "Learn more about Zn anodes from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") utilization efficiency and facilitating industrial applications. Nevertheless, the unique spherical microstructure of Zn-p introduces specific challenges, such as complex ion pathways, uneven Zn deposition, and a complicated electrode manufacturing process, which directly impact the [battery](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/battery-electrochemical-energy-engineering "Learn more about battery from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") performance, safety, and lifespan. [In](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/indium "Learn more about In from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") pursuit of a deeply comprehensive relationship between [electrode structure](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/electrode-structure "Learn more about electrode structure from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") and electrochemical performance for Zn-p-based anodes, this review provides a detailed examination of the working mechanisms of Zn-p-based anodes, while also identifying both the opportunities they present and the unique challenges they face. Moreover, the latest research progress is summarized, with a focus on innovative design strategies for optimizing Zn-p-based anodes. The relationship between these structural aspects and the resulting electrochemical performance, including safety, cycle life, and capacity, is thoroughly examined to provide a deeper understanding of how to achieve optimal [battery](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/battery-electrochemical-energy-engineering "Learn more about battery from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") system design and performance. Meanwhile, the fabrication processes and potential for practical flexible applications of Zn-p-based batteries were detailed. Finally, prospects for achieving high performance and practical utilization of Zn-p-based anodes are offered, providing scientific guidance for their practical application.  

## Introduction  

The development of efficient and reliable energy storage systems, particularly rechargeable batteries for renewable and green energy sources, is crucial in addressing the excessive reliance on fossil fuels and mitigating environmental pollution [1]. Since the initial market introduction in 1991 [2], rechargeable lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) have established themselves as trustworthy energy storage devices for consumer electronics and hold promise for electric vehicles. However, due to the flammability of organic electrolytes [3,4] and the limited availability of lithium [5,6], it is an urgent need for battery technologies that are inherently safe, cost-effective, and environmentally friendly, especially for large-scale stationary grid energy storage [7].  

Research has explored alternatives to LIBs, focusing on aqueous batteries that utilize cost-effective alkali metal cations such as sodium ion (Na+) and potassium ion (K+), as well as multivalent metal cations such as magnesium ion (Mg2+), aluminum ion (Al3+), and zinc ion (Zn2+) [8,9]. These aqueous electrolytes not only eliminate the need for metallic lithium but also offer significantly higher ionic conductivity compared to organic electrolytes and have intrinsic safety features, making them suitable for a variety of energy storage applications [10]. However, during the (de)intercalation process of multivalent metal ions, the host lattice experiences varying degrees of contraction and expansion, particularly in cases of excessive ion intercalation or co-intercalation of metal ions with H+ or water (H2O) molecules. This induces significant mechanical stress, which can destabilize and ultimately cause the collapse of layered-structure materials, further aggravating the problem of capacity decay [11,12]. Among these alternatives, zinc-ion batteries (ZIBs) have garnered growing interest from both academia and industry [13] due to their high theoretical capacity of 820 mAh g−1 (5854 mAh cm−3), low redox potential (-0.763 V vs. standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)), natural abundance, and environmental friendliness [14,15].  

In recent years, the architectural designs of rechargeable aqueous zinc-ion batteries (AZIBs) have become increasingly diverse. As depicted in Fig. 1, the typical AZIBs structure includes a cathode, anode, separator, and electrolyte [16,17]. The anode plays a crucial role in defining the performance, durability, and lifespan of AZIBs, with metallic Zn widely considered an ideal anode material. Commercially, Zn foils, typically 100 or 200 μm thick, are commonly used as anodes due to their manufacturing maturity and reliability. However, the excessive capacity and thickness of these Zn anodes result in a low depth of discharge (DOD) when paired with the cathode. In addition, the surplus mass of the Zn foil results in a significant detriment to the energy density. For practical metal batteries, it is crucial to effectively pair them with metal oxide cathodes that have an areal capacity of 3 to 4 mAh cm−2. This pairing necessitates the use of an ultra-thin Zn foil, no thicker than 8.5 μm, to achieve a corresponding Zn metal capacity of 5 mAh cm−2. However, continuous side reactions that consume a limited amount of Zn exacerbate this issue, especially for Zn foil as thin as 8.5 μm, leading to premature electrode failure. Additionally, producing such thin Zn foils is more challenging and incurs higher costs. Currently, the thinnest commercially available Zn foil is 20 μm, which still exceeds actual capacity requirements.

This review aims to address critical challenges by focusing on an alternative material-zinc powder (Zn-p), which demonstrates significant advantages over traditional Zn foil in terms of cost-effectiveness, large-scale processing, and enhanced performance in various energy storage applications. The Zn-p, commonly used as an anode material in dry batteries, is recognized for its unique spherical microstructure, affordability, and scalability, making it a practical and promising alternative to conventional anodes [18]. Recent researches in Zn-p-based anodes have demonstrated significant improvements in performance and stability. The large surface area allows for more efficient electrochemical reactions, enhancing charge and discharge rates, and facilitating faster Zn2+ ions transport. In addition, the gaps between Zn-p provide expansion buffering, preventing stress concentration and crack formation, while multi-directional stress distribution reduces single-point stress. The compatibility with flexible materials also offers the potential for flexible or wearable batteries. The granular structure of Zn-p also allows for the adjustment of particle size distribution and dosage, optimizing the negative/positive (N/P) mass ratio and enhancing Zn anode utilization [19]. Despite these numerous advantages, Zn-p-based anodes face several significant challenges except for the conventional issues such as dendrite growth and side reactions, including rapidly increasing polarization voltage with the number of cycles, the severe gas production and cell swelling, the electrode chalking, the electrical contact brought about by the use of additives. In addition, the variations in processing methods can lead to significant differences in electrode performance.

Therefore, this review presents a comprehensive analysis of the opportunities and challenges of Zn-p-based anodes for ZIBs, clarifying the operational mechanisms and highlighting the advanced anode design strategies for optimal battery performance. In addition, the advantages and disadvantages of the manufacturing process of Zn-p-based anodes are investigated in detail. Based on these, this work evaluates the potential of Zn-p-based anodes and provides deep insights into their capabilities to advance energy storage systems. The timely publication of this review not only will guide future research efforts but is also expected to significantly propel advancements in the field of energy storage.

## Manufacturing techniques

AZIBs are characterized by their low cost, high operational safety, and ecological friendliness, making them ideal for large-scale energy storage systems. The mainstream approach for fabricating electrodes in AZIBs involves a coating process, adept at meeting the production requirements for standard cylindrical and soft-pack batteries. However, this method is inefficient when it comes to manufacturing batteries with non-traditional shapes and sizes, such as ultra-micro or ultra-large batteries.
